You are designing a photo frame for a Snapbox photo booth. It can be a photo strip,
a big landscape sheet or a big portrait sheet. Follow this spec exactly. The booth
turns away any design that breaks the hard rules.

WHAT TO PRODUCE
ONE file: a PNG picture of the artwork, with a fully transparent rectangle
wherever a photo goes. Nothing else is needed: the booth finds the photo boxes by
looking for the transparent rectangles.
Draw it with code (Python + Pillow works well) so the sizes are exact. Image
generators cannot hit exact pixel sizes or true transparency. If you can run code,
run it and give me the PNG to download. If not, give me one script that writes it.

HOW TO HAND IT OVER
- Give me the PNG itself as a download link. Do NOT put it in a zip or any other
  archive, and do not send a JSON or any second file: I add it to the booth from
  my phone, which can only pick one loose PNG.
- Name the file after the design, in lowercase letters, digits and dashes
  (party-night.png). The booth uses the file name as the design's name.
- If I ask for more than one design or layout, give each its own PNG, its own name
  and its own download link (party-night-strip.png, party-night-wide.png, ...).

HARD RULES (all three layouts; the booth checks every one)
- The PNG is exactly the size for its layout, has an alpha channel (RGBA) and is
  under 20 MB.
- 1 to 8 photo boxes. Each is a plain rectangle, fully transparent (alpha 0 on
  every pixel inside it), at least 60 x 60 pixels (far bigger in practice) and
  fully inside the canvas.
- ONLY the photo boxes are transparent. Every other pixel is fully opaque (alpha
  255). No transparent background, margin, border or gap anywhere: where you want
  white, paint white. A transparent area that is not a photo box makes the booth
  turn the design away.
- Photo boxes do not touch each other: leave at least 20 px of opaque frame
  between them, or the booth sees them as one box.
- Nothing is drawn inside a photo box. Do not lay tape, stickers or text across one.
- Photos fill the boxes in reading order: top to bottom, and left to right within
  a row. Arrange the boxes so that order makes sense.

HOW THE BOOTH USES IT
- Each photo is 16:9 landscape. It is center-cropped to fill its box, then the PNG
  is laid on top. Wide boxes keep most of the photo. Tall boxes, common on portrait
  sheets, keep only the middle (a 460 x 600 box keeps under half the width).
- The photo extends 5 px past the box on every side, so no gap shows at the edge.
- If the date stamp is on, it sits in the bottom right corner of each photo. The
  digits are about 7.5% of the box height, inset about 8% of the box height from
  the bottom and 6% of the box's longer side from the right. Keep art away from
  that corner of each box's edge.

DESIGN TIPS
- Keep text and important art at least 60 px from every edge. The printer trims a little.
- Make text at least 40 px tall with strong contrast. A strip is only 2 inches wide.
- A thin border or mat around each photo box makes the photos look finished.
- Sheets have room for a big title, names and artwork. Strips do not: keep strip
  text short.
